Afyonkarahisar Food Guide

Famous for its clotted cream, Turkish delight and thermal waters, Afyonkarahisar is a gastronomy city beneath a castle on a rock.

A UNESCO City of Gastronomy

Afyonkarahisar was declared a gastronomy city on October 31, 2019, the third Turkish city after Gaziantep and Hatay. According to its mayor, it has nearly 600 local delicacies, and nearly 30 of them are registered with geographical indication certificates.

Afyon kaymak

Afyon kaymak (clotted cream) received a geographical indication registration in 2008. Its main raw material is buffalo milk. It is eaten at breakfast, in ekmek kadayıfı and inside Turkish delight.

Lokum, sausage and ekmek kadayıfı

Kaymak lokum, Afyon sausage and ekmek kadayıfı are among the region's best-known foods. Ekmek kadayıfı is a syrup-soaked bread dessert served with kaymak.

Castle and thermal waters

The city is also known for the castle that rises from a rock and for its thermal waters, drawing visitors for history, wellness and food.

How kaymak is made

Afyon kaymak is made by carefully boiling and then cooling buffalo milk. According to one producer, making it takes about 36 hours. The quality standard requires buffalo milk, and some producers mixing in cow's milk has been the subject of studies. A local official says about 253 tons of buffalo cream are produced yearly across 17 businesses in the province.

Kaymak at the table

At breakfast it is sliced and served with honey. Ekmek kadayıfı is eaten with kaymak; a good one should not be dry inside, should not fall apart when sliced, and should let the cream's own taste come through. Kaymaklı börek, kaymak dolması (a winter specialty) and the geographically indicated keşkek, cooked in a clay pot in a stone oven, pounded and served with buffalo cream, are other highlights.

Afyon sausage

Afyon sausage is usually eaten at breakfast, fried in a pan or with eggs.

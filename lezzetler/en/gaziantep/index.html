Gaziantep Food Guide

Gaziantep, Türkiye's city of gastronomy, is famous for baklava, pistachios and a rich table.

A UNESCO City of Gastronomy

Gaziantep joined UNESCO's Creative Cities Network for gastronomy in December 2015 and is described as the first Turkish city to do so. Its cuisine, sweet and savory, is among the richest in the country.

Antep baklava

Gaziantep baklava gained protected geographical status in the European Union in 2013. Thin sheets of dough, plenty of Antep pistachios and syrup make it the city's best-known symbol.

Beyran and katmer

Antep beyran, a spiced traditional meat dish, was registered by TÜRKPATENT as a geographical sign in 2017. Antep katmer, a pistachio-and-cream pastry, was registered the same year.

Pistachios and other protected foods

Antep pistachio is a separate geographical indication. Araban garlic, menengiç coffee and pistachio paste are cited among Gaziantep's EU-registered products, and börek soup, içli köfte and Antep cookies are registered too.

How baklava is made

According to sources, baklava is built from dozens of thin sheets of dough; depending on the recipe, 40, 60 or more layers may be used. Each layer is brushed with melted butter or ghee, with a semolina cream and plenty of ground Antep pistachios between them. After baking, a syrup of sugar, water and lemon is poured over it and the baklava rests.

Why pistachios?

Walnuts were the affordable filling at first; as pistachio farming grew in the region, pistachios became the standard filling around the mid-19th century. Today the Antep pistachio is the symbol of both baklava and the city.

Frequently asked questions

What is Gaziantep's most famous dessert?

Antep baklava, protected as a geographical indication in the EU since 2013.

Why is Gaziantep a city of gastronomy?

It joined UNESCO's Creative Cities Network for gastronomy in December 2015.

What should I eat in Gaziantep?

Baklava, beyran, katmer, börek soup, içli köfte and desserts made with Antep pistachios.

How many layers does baklava have?

It depends on the recipe; sources mention 40, 60 or more layers of dough.
